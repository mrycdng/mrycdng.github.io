my memory of my old home is no longer tangible; it slips through my fingers as readily, as formless as water. I don’t recall the tree outside of my building any clearer than the abstract notion of “tree”. my memory is planks of rotten wood. And so I remember guangzhou like this—“trees” and scorching “sunlight“ and “cars” and old “apartment“ buildings and “uncles” in flip flops and nothing beyond. On google streetview my home is a gaping chasm. Two 360 videos clumsily taken by a local and a tourist on the fringes of my neighborhood, places I’m not sure I’ve ever stepped foot in. Baidu (the Chinese equivalent of google) is better but not by much. I go forwards and forwards until i face a stretch of road their car never turned onto. I zoom in, then, to stare at the pixels that constitute my old apartment. images are a privilege. My ship is a ghostly existence on open water. There is no driftwood anywhere.
